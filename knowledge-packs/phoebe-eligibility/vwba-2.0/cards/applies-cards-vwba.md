Two cards. Together they are the **"does this apply" test** for the water pack: they say
whether the volumetric-water-benefit pathway is in play for the project in front of
Phoebe. **They never say eligible.** A pathway that applies still has all six criteria to
meet. All wording is a rewrite; no sentence is copied from the source. The source PDF stays
in `sources-local/`, which is gitignored and never publishes; this file cites and links it.

## How the test is run, and what it is not

**Two yes-or-no questions.** Both yes and the water pathway applies: the six-criteria
section of the worksheet opens, exactly as it does today. A no is sorted before anything is
ruled out, under the guide-not-gate rule of 23 Sep 2026: each card's "If the answer is no"
says which kind it is — Fixable, with a cited route from `routes-cards-vwba.md`; Unknown, not
a fail; or Blocked, where no cited route exists — and Phoebe says so once and goes on with
whatever other pathway is in play.

**The record first.** What the visitor told Wellington in "what it does" usually settles
W1 without a question, and often W2. Phoebe asks only for what those words leave open.

**Questions cover both pathways where one answer can.** Maintainer's ruling of 22 Sep 2026
(R4). "What does the project do, in a sentence?" answers W1 here and T1 on the carbon pack
at once. A verdict covers every card and row the answer settles.

**Why there is no version card.** One version of the guidebook is cited and it carries no
transition rule; nothing sorts a project by when it began.

**Not a verdict, not a count.** The two cards carry no state beyond *applies* or *does not
apply* for the pathway.

## Card W1 — A water stewardship activity with a volume behind it

**The test in plain words.** The project is a water stewardship activity: work done in a
catchment, outside the sponsor's own walls, that either changes how water moves through
that catchment for the better, or helps ease a water problem the people and bodies there
share, or both. And the good it does can be put as a volume of water over a period of
time. That volume is what the guidebook calls a volumetric water benefit, and the whole
pathway rests on there being one to count. Work that does neither of those things, or
whose good cannot be put as a volume, is not this pathway, however worthwhile it is.

**Source.** VWBA 2.0 (Version 1, September 2025), Glossary, "volumetric water benefits
(VWBs)" and "water stewardship", p. 66; Appendix A, criterion 1 — "Established pathway for
a quantifiable VWB", p. 32.

**Canonical link.** <https://doi.org/10.46830/wrigb.23.00112> — the publisher's record for
this guidebook, World Resources Institute. Not a mirror or a re-hosted copy.

**Applies to.** Every project asking about this pathway.

**What a project owner would be asked to show.**

- What the activity does on the ground, in a sentence.
- Which shared water problem it takes on, or how it changes the water picture, in the
  owner's words.
- Whether anyone expects to state the result as a volume of water, and roughly how.

**If the answer is no.** Phoebe sorts the no before she says anything is out. *Fixable* when
the project does something for the water but nobody has named the shared challenge or the
volume: route R-2 finds the challenge from the catchment, route R-3 picks the indicator and method,
and route R-1 reworks the scope with the implementer. *Blocked* on the water pathway only when the
activity does nothing to the water of any catchment at all; no cited route changes that, and the
carbon pathway may still apply, so she goes on with T1. *Unknown* when the visitor cannot yet say
what the activity does for the water: not a fail; she asks once and leaves it Unknown.

## Card W2 — A mapped route to counting it

**The test in plain words.** There is a way to work the volume out. The guidebook keeps a
table of the most common project activities, and for each one it names what to measure and
which method counts it. If the project's activity is in that table, the route is mapped.
A project that gives households or a community access to drinking water sits in the
table's water, sanitation and hygiene row and maps to the Volume Provided method, D-3.
If the activity is not in the table, it is not thereby ruled out: the route has to be
established some other way, with a method resting on the same principles, and that is a
question for criterion 1 rather than a reason to stop here.

**Source.** VWBA 2.0 (Version 1, September 2025), Appendix C, "Guidance for indicator and
method selection", Table C-1, pp. 37–39, the WASH row; Step 3, p. 21, which sends a
practitioner to that table; Appendix A, criterion 1, p. 32.

**Canonical link.** <https://doi.org/10.46830/wrigb.23.00112> — the publisher's record for
this guidebook, World Resources Institute. Not a mirror or a re-hosted copy.

**Applies to.** Every project asking about this pathway.

**What a project owner would be asked to show.**

- The kind of activity, in words the table would recognise: new water supply, treatment,
  leak repair, recharge, restoration, and so on.
- Where they expect the number to come from, if they already know.
- For an activity not in the table: what method they would use and why it holds to the
  same principles.

**If the answer is no.** Never *Blocked*: the guidebook says an activity outside its table is
not ruled out (Appendix C, p. 39). *Fixable* when the activity is in the table but the route has
not been chosen: route R-3, and route R-8 for a drinking-water project. *Unknown* when the
activity is outside the table and no method has been named yet: not a fail. She says the route
has to be established under criterion 1 with a method resting on the same principles, points at
the subject matter expert the guidebook names for that choice (Step 3, p. 21), and offers the save
door.
