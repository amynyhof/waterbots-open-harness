**For Phoebe (beta) — Eligibility and Feasibility specialist. Pack `gs-paa-v2.0`.**

**Status: approved by the maintainer, 23 Sep 2026.** Drafted at the repository root on 22 Sep
2026, its "If the answer is no" sections redrafted under the guide-not-gate rule on 23 Sep, graded
"passes as redrafted" with one addition on card T4, and moved here whole on her word. Item K7,

Four cards. Together they are the **"does this apply" test** for this pack: they say whether
the carbon pathway is in play for the project in front of Phoebe. **They never say
eligible.** A pathway that applies still has every worksheet row to meet. All wording is a
rewrite; no sentence is copied from a source. The source PDFs stay in `sources-local/`,
which is gitignored and never publishes; this file cites and links them.

---

## How the test is run, and what it is not

**Three yes-or-no questions and one sorting question.** Cards T1, T2 and T3 each take a
yes or a no. Three yeses and the pathway applies: its section of the worksheet opens. A no
is sorted before anything is ruled out, under the guide-not-gate rule of 23 Sep 2026: each
card's "If the answer is no" says which kind it is — Fixable, with a cited route; Unknown,
not a fail; or Blocked, where no cited route exists — and Phoebe says so once and goes on
with whatever other pathway is in play. Card T4 is not yes-or-no; it sets which version of
the methodology the project is judged on, and that flag changes what some rows ask for.

**The record first.** What the visitor told Wellington, in "what it does" and "where it is",
often settles T1 to T3 without a question. Phoebe asks only for what those words leave
open. The "what kind" field is a hint from the visitor, never the test's answer.

**Questions cover both pathways where one answer can.** Maintainer's ruling of 22 Sep 2026
(R4). "What does the project do, in a sentence?" answers T1 here and W1 on the water pack
at once. A verdict covers every card and row the answer settles.

**Not a verdict, not a count.** The four cards carry no state of their own beyond
*applies* or *does not apply* for the pathway. There is no "three of four".

## Card T1 — Safe drinking water from a technology that gives off little or nothing

**The test in plain words.** The activity puts in a new technology, or brings an existing
one back into service, whose job is to supply drinking water that is safe to drink, and
the technology itself gives off no greenhouse gas as it runs, or only a little (for
example, a pump on grid electricity). A gravity filter and a handpump are the plain cases
of zero. Anything that runs on its own fossil-fuel engine is not this.

**Source.** GS4GG PAA M400-12 (Version 2.0, 9 July 2026), §3.1.1 "Scope", p. 8; §3.1.4,
p. 9; Table 2, "Low-emission technology", p. 7.

**Canonical link.** <https://globalgoals.goldstandard.org/429-ee-sws-emission-reductions-from-safe-drinking-water-supply/>
— the publisher's page for this methodology. Not a mirror or a re-hosted copy.

**Applies to.** Every project asking about this pathway.

**What a project owner would be asked to show.**

- What the technology is, in a sentence: what it does to the water, or where the water
  comes from.
- What powers it, if anything: nothing, sunlight, the grid, or an engine.
- Whether it is new, or an existing system being restored.

**If the answer is no.** Phoebe sorts the no before she says anything is out. *Blocked* on the
carbon pathway if the activity does not supply drinking water at all: the method counts nothing
else (§3.1.1, p. 8), no cited route changes that, and the water pathway may still apply, so she
goes on with W1. *Fixable* if the only fault is a pump on its own fossil-fuel engine: the method
names the technologies that do qualify, handpumps, solar and grid pumps, so the route is a change
of pump (§3.2.1.1 b and §3.2.1.2, p. 9); the carbon routes card for it is not drafted yet, and she
says so. *Unknown* if the visitor cannot say what powers it: she asks, and not knowing is not a
fail.

## Card T2 — Today, people boil, or go without

**The test in plain words.** The people the project will serve get their drinking water
today in one of two ways the method counts. Either they boil unsafe water over wood,
charcoal or a fossil fuel, so that safe water from the project would stop that burning.
Or they drink unsafe water, or too little safe water, because they cannot afford fuel or
safe water, or no service reaches them; the method calls this suppressed demand, and it
is what a small or micro-scale project may count. People who already have safe water from
an improved source are not a baseline the method counts, unless that source can be shown
to fail the safe-water definition.

**Source.** GS4GG PAA M400-12 (Version 2.0, 9 July 2026), §3.1.2, p. 8; §3.2.4.1,
p. 10; §7.3.1 "Baseline scenario", p. 26; parameter SDWS 12, pp. 52–53.

**Canonical link.** <https://globalgoals.goldstandard.org/429-ee-sws-emission-reductions-from-safe-drinking-water-supply/>
— the publisher's page for this methodology. Not a mirror or a re-hosted copy.

**Applies to.** Every project asking about this pathway.

**What a project owner would be asked to show.**

- How the people served get drinking water now, in their own words: boiled, and over
  what; or untreated; or bought; or from a tap.
- Whether the source they use today counts as improved (a protected well, a borehole, a
  piped supply) and, if so, whether there is evidence it is unsafe all the same.
- Roughly how many people, so the scale question later has something to rest on.

**If the answer is no.** *Blocked* on the carbon pathway when the people served already have
safe water and nobody boils or goes without: there is no fuel use to stop and no suppressed demand
to meet, and no cited route makes one (§3.1.2, p. 8; §7.3.1, p. 26). She says that plainly and
goes on with the water pathway, which may still apply. *Unknown* when the visitor does not know
how people get water today: not a fail. The method's own answer is a baseline survey, and Gold
Standard publishes the questionnaires for it (§7.3.4, p. 27; SDWS 5, pp. 49–50; workbooks
429.3–429.12), so she names that route, sets Unknown, and offers the save door.

## Card T3 — One of four kinds of technology

**The test in plain words.** The technology is one of the four classes the method knows.
**Household treatment (HWT):** the water is treated in the home, at the point where it is
drunk, by a filter, chlorine, or UV light. **Institutional treatment (IWT):** the same,
on the premises of a school, clinic, prison, or camp. **Community treatment (CWT):** the
water is treated at one central point and then piped to homes or collected there, at a
kiosk or a tap. **Community supply (CWS):** the project makes a supply of water for a
community, such as a new or restored borehole handpump, a protected spring, or a solar
pump; if that water is not safe on its own, the supply has to be paired with treatment of
one of the other three kinds. Which class it is decides which worksheet rows open: a home
filter is never asked about boreholes.

**Source.** GS4GG PAA M400-12 (Version 2.0, 9 July 2026), Table 2 "Terms and
definitions", pp. 6–7; Table 3 "Applicable technologies and calculation methods", p. 9;
§3.2.1.1 "Eligible technologies", p. 9.

**Canonical link.** <https://globalgoals.goldstandard.org/429-ee-sws-emission-reductions-from-safe-drinking-water-supply/>
— the publisher's page for this methodology. Not a mirror or a re-hosted copy.

**Applies to.** Every project asking about this pathway. The answer sets the class flag
the eligibility rows read: `HWT`, `IWT`, `CWT`, or `CWS`.

**What a project owner would be asked to show.**

- Where the water is treated or produced: in each home, at an institution, at one
  community point, or at a source such as a well or spring.
- How people get it from there: they treat it themselves, it is piped, or they collect it.
- For a supply project, whether the water is safe as it comes out, or is treated as well.

**If the answer is no.** *Blocked* on the carbon pathway when the technology is none of the
four classes: the method covers nothing else (§3.2.1.1, p. 9; Annex 5 §A5.3.1 c, p. 100), no cited
route exists, and she says so and offers to send the project to a person. *Unknown* when the
visitor cannot yet say where the water is treated or produced: not a fail. She names the four
classes in plain words, asks once, and leaves the row Unknown if they still do not know.

## Card T4 — Which version the project is judged on

**The test in plain words.** Two kinds of project reach this pathway, and the method
treats them differently in timing, not in substance. **A new project**, not yet
registered with Gold Standard, is judged on the 2026 version of the methodology, the one
aligned with the Paris Agreement, because any new project submitted after 30 June 2026
has to use a Paris-aligned methodology, and only credits earned under one may be dated
2026 or later. **A transitioning project**, already certified under the 2021 version,
keeps earning credits dated up to the end of 2025 on the old version, but earns nothing
dated 2026 or later until it has made the move: a revised design and monitoring plan,
checked by an approved auditor and reviewed by Gold Standard. The renewal of its
crediting period is the point where the move is compulsory. A transitioning project is
judged on the same worksheet rows as a new one, on a timetable the auditor's requirements
set for this method: the regulatory-surplus and lock-in rows are re-run at once, before
any 2026 credit; the water-quality, common-practice and baseline-survey rows are re-run
at the renewal.

**Source.** *Requirements for Paris Agreement Alignment*, PR100-01 (Version 1.1,
2 February 2026, effective 1 January 2026), §3.1.1, p. 5; §4.1.1–4.1.3, p. 6;
§4.3.2–4.3.3, p. 7; §4.4.1, p. 9. *PAA Validation and Verification Requirements for
Methodologies Update*, VVS100-01 (Version 2.0, 10 July 2026), Annex 5, §A5.1.1–A5.1.4,
p. 44, and Table A5-1, pp. 45–47, the safe-water transition matrix. GS4GG PAA M400-12
(Version 2.0, 9 July 2026), §17.1.1, p. 79, the latest version applies at renewal.

**Canonical link.** <https://globalgoals.goldstandard.org/119_paa_pr_100-01> — the
publisher's page for the Paris-alignment requirements. Second link, for the transition
matrix: <https://globalgoals.goldstandard.org/120_paa-vvs100-01-paa-validation-and-verification-requirements-for-methodologies-update/>.
The 2021 methodology a transitioning project came in on is listed on the methodology's
own page, cited on cards T1 to T3. None is a mirror or a re-hosted copy.

**Applies to.** Every project on this pathway. The answer sets the version flag the
eligibility rows read: `new` or `transitioning`.

**What a project owner would be asked to show.**

- Whether the project is already registered or certified with Gold Standard, and if so
  under which version of this methodology.
- For a transitioning project: whether the PA-alignment design change has been made, is
  under way, or has not started; and when the current crediting period ends.
- For a new project: whether it has been submitted for preliminary review, and when.

**If the answer is unknown.** "I don't know" is an answer, and it is *Unknown*, not a fail.
Phoebe treats the project as new, says she has done so, and names the one fact that would change
it: a Gold Standard registration under the 2021 version. There is a cited route to that fact:
every Gold Standard project is public on the Impact Registry from listing onward (Principles &
Requirements v2.1 §2.2.2, p. 5), so the owner can look the project up by name.

**For a transitioning project, a fact Phoebe phrases.** Maintainer's ruling, 23 Sep 2026: a
transition-assistance module, overseen by trusted consultants, is coming to the paid site; the
visitor can save the project and sign up for access. Said once, as a fact, where card T4 finds a
transitioning project; never a sales line, and never volunteered anywhere else.
