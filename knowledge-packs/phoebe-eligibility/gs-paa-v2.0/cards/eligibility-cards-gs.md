## How these seventeen sit with the water cards, and with each other

**The same three kinds of miss.** A row not met today is Fixable, with a cited route; Unknown, with
what to find out; or Blocked, only where the card's line allows. The readiness read for the
carbon pathway is drawn from these rows and the fifteen to follow, never from prose.

**Not every row opens for every project.** Each card's **Applies to** names the technology class,
scale or version flag it binds. A household filter project never hears about boreholes; a
community supply never hears about the household performance bar. The class comes from applies
card T3, the version flag from T4.

**The carbon routes are not drafted yet.** Where a card says a fix exists, it cites the section of
the methodology the fix rests on and names the route the proposal planned (its §5.4); the route
cards themselves come with the second half of step 4. Until then a route named here is a source,
not a card Phoebe can render.

**Where an external standard owns the number**, the card says so under **External standard**,
and sends the reader to it rather than reciting a table (CITATIONS.md, number-dense passages).

## Card M1 — A technology the method names

**The rule in plain words.** The method counts only technologies it names. For treating water:
ceramic, sand, composite or membrane filters; chlorine or bleach dosing; UV light. For supplying
water: a new or restored borehole handpump, a protected spring, or a mechanised pump. Anything else
is outside the method, however well it works.

**Can it be fixed? Depends, on one fact: whether the technology can be one the method names.** No
evidence makes an unlisted technology listed; only a change of technology does. Phoebe asks
whether the design could use a listed one; Blocked only when the visitor says it will not change.
Route: the list itself (§3.2.1.1, p. 9); planned carbon route R-2 covers the household case.

**Phase.** To start.

**Source.** GS4GG PAA M400-12 (Version 2.0, 9 July 2026), §3.2.1.1 "Eligible technologies",
p. 9; Table 3, p. 9.

**Canonical link.** <https://globalgoals.goldstandard.org/429-ee-sws-emission-reductions-from-safe-drinking-water-supply/>
— the publisher's page for this methodology. Not a mirror or a re-hosted copy.

**Applies to.** All.

**External standard.** None.

**What a project owner would be asked to show.**

- The technology, named the way the method names it.
- For a supply project, whether the water is safe as it comes out or is treated as well.

## Card M2 — No pump on its own fossil-fuel engine

**The rule in plain words.** A pump driven by its own diesel or petrol engine is not eligible. A
fossil-fuel back-up behind an electric or solar pump is allowed only while it runs for a tenth or
less of the main technology's operating hours; the hours are logged. Across Gold Standard,
energy from fossil fuel is excluded unless a methodology carves it out, and this one carves out
only the back-up.

**Can it be fixed? Depends, on the pump.** A fossil engine as the main drive is out; the route is
a change of drive to a handpump, solar or grid pump (§3.2.1.1 b, p. 9). A back-up over the tenth
is fixed by running it less and keeping the log. Blocked only when the visitor says the engine
stays as the main drive. Planned carbon route: a change of pump, with M1's list as its source.

**Phase.** To start. Kept up by: the back-up engine's hours logged through the crediting period.

**Source.** GS4GG PAA M400-12 (Version 2.0, 9 July 2026), §3.2.1.2 "Fossil fuel restrictions",
p. 9; parameter SDWS 38, pp. 72–73. Also *Principles & Requirements* (v2.1, 31 January 2025),
§4.1.7, p. 8.

**Canonical link.** <https://globalgoals.goldstandard.org/429-ee-sws-emission-reductions-from-safe-drinking-water-supply/>
— the publisher's page for this methodology. Second link, for the general rule:
<https://globalgoals.goldstandard.org/101-par-principles-requirements/>. Neither is a mirror or a
re-hosted copy.

**Applies to.** CWS, and any system with a powered pump or a back-up engine.

**External standard.** None.

**What a project owner would be asked to show.**

- What drives the pump, and whether there is a back-up engine.
- For a back-up, its running hours against the main technology's, logged.

## Card M3 — The treatment technology is shown to work

**The rule in plain words.** A household or institutional treatment technology has to be shown to
meet a national standard for household water treatment, or, where the country has none, the WHO
International Scheme's two-star or three-star level, the ones the Scheme calls comprehensive
protection. The showing is a lab test report or an official notice; an expert's opinion does not
count. The test conditions have to look like the real site, including the water going in.

**Can it be fixed? Yes.** Get the product's lab report or its listing under the Scheme, or, where
the country has a standard, its notice. A technology with no such paper has work to do, not a
wall; a technology that fails the bar is fixed by choosing one that passes. Route source:
§3.2.2.1 and SDWS 2; planned carbon route R-2.

**Phase.** To start.

**Source.** GS4GG PAA M400-12 (Version 2.0, 9 July 2026), §3.2.2.1 "Technology performance",
pp. 9–10; parameter SDWS 2, p. 47.

**Canonical link.** <https://globalgoals.goldstandard.org/429-ee-sws-emission-reductions-from-safe-drinking-water-supply/>
— the publisher's page for this methodology. Not a mirror or a re-hosted copy.

**Applies to.** HWT, IWT.

**External standard.** The WHO International Scheme to Evaluate Household Water Treatment
Technologies sets the star levels: three stars means the product removes at least 4 log of
bacteria, 5 log of viruses and 4 log of protozoa; two stars at least 2, 3 and 2 (*Results of
Rounds III and IV*, published 18 March 2026, ISBN 978-92-4-011212-4, Table E1, p. ix; §2.2.2, p. 6). A national standard, where one exists,
comes first. The figures are the Scheme's; the card sends the reader there.

**What a project owner would be asked to show.**

- The maker, the product and the type.
- The lab report, the Scheme listing, or the national notice.

## Card M4 — The water at the collection point is safe

**The rule in plain words.** For a community treatment or supply system, the water where people
collect it has to meet two kinds of standard: microbial, by the national standard or, if there is
none, the WHO guideline values; and chemical and physical, by the country's priority contaminants
such as arsenic and fluoride, or international guidance where the country has none. It is shown at
the start of each crediting period and tested again after anything that could foul the source,
such as a flood. The lab is accredited or approved by the health authority.

**Can it be fixed? Yes.** Test the water; where it fails, treat it, or pair the supply with a
treatment technology, then test again. Route source: §3.2.2.2 and SDWS 3; planned carbon route
R-3.

**Phase.** To start. Kept up by: retesting after any event that could foul the source, and at each renewal.

**Source.** GS4GG PAA M400-12 (Version 2.0, 9 July 2026), §3.2.2.2, p. 10; parameter SDWS 3,
pp. 47–48.

**Canonical link.** <https://globalgoals.goldstandard.org/429-ee-sws-emission-reductions-from-safe-drinking-water-supply/>
— the publisher's page for this methodology. Not a mirror or a re-hosted copy.

**Applies to.** CWT, CWS.

**External standard.** WHO *Guidelines for Drinking-water Quality*, fourth edition: the microbial
bar is E. coli not detectable in any 100 ml sample (Table 7.10, 2017 edition p. 149; unchanged in
the 2022 and 2026 editions), and the guideline values for arsenic (10 µg/L) and fluoride
(1.5 mg/L). A national standard, where one exists, comes first.

**What a project owner would be asked to show.**

- Test results from an accredited or approved lab, or an approved field kit with its checks.
- The date of the test against the start of the crediting period.

## Card M5 — The people served boil today, or go without

**The rule in plain words.** The method counts two kinds of people. Those who boil unsafe water
today over wood, charcoal or a fossil fuel, so that safe water would stop the burning; and those
who drink unsafe water, or too little safe water, because they cannot afford fuel or safe water or
no service reaches them, which the method calls suppressed demand. People who already have safe
water from an improved source are not counted, unless that source is shown to fail the safe-water
definition by tests over six months or literature no older than three years.

**Can it be fixed? Depends, on what the people served do today.** Nobody boils and nobody goes
without: Blocked, because there is nothing to count and no route makes it so; the water pathway
may still apply. Not known yet: Unknown, not a fail, and the method's own answer is a baseline
survey, with Gold Standard's questionnaires published for it. An improved source suspected unsafe:
Fixable, by testing it. Route source: §7.3.4 and SDWS 5; planned carbon route R-1 (the baseline
survey) and R-4 (showing a source is unsafe).

**Phase.** To start.

**Source.** GS4GG PAA M400-12 (Version 2.0, 9 July 2026), §3.1.2, p. 8; §3.2.4.1, p. 10;
§7.3.1 "Baseline scenario", p. 26; §7.3.4, p. 27; parameters SDWS 5, pp. 49–50, and SDWS 12,
pp. 52–53.

**Canonical link.** <https://globalgoals.goldstandard.org/429-ee-sws-emission-reductions-from-safe-drinking-water-supply/>
— the publisher's page for this methodology. Not a mirror or a re-hosted copy.

**Applies to.** All.

**External standard.** The WHO/UNICEF JMP definitions of improved and unimproved sources (*Core
questions on water, sanitation and hygiene for household surveys*, 2018 update, p. 9), which the
method points at in its Annex 4.

**What a project owner would be asked to show.**

- How the people served get drinking water now, in their own words.
- Whether the source is improved and, if so, evidence it is unsafe all the same.
- A baseline survey, or the plan to run one.

## Card M6 — Suppressed demand only for small and micro-scale projects

**The rule in plain words.** Only a micro-scale project, up to 10,000 tonnes of CO2e a year, or a
small-scale one, up to 60,000, may count suppressed demand. A large-scale project may count only
people shown to be boiling before the project. The general rules say the same: suppressed-demand
baselines are for small scale, and a project using one may not stack other products.

**Can it be fixed? Depends, on the project's scale.** A large project can still count its
boilers, or be designed at small scale; a project that stays large and has no boilers has nothing
to count on this row. Blocked only when the visitor says the project stays large and nobody boils.
Route source: §3.2.4.2 and the scale lines of Table 1.

**Phase.** To start.

**Source.** GS4GG PAA M400-12 (Version 2.0, 9 July 2026), §3.2.4.2, p. 10; Table 1, p. 5. Also
*Principles & Requirements* (v2.1, 31 January 2025), §4.1.10, p. 9; *Community Services Activity
Requirements* (v1.2, October 2019), §3.1.3, p. 5; *GHG Emissions Reduction & Sequestration
Product Requirements* (v3.2, 12 December 2025), §9.1.5, p. 11.

**Canonical link.** <https://globalgoals.goldstandard.org/429-ee-sws-emission-reductions-from-safe-drinking-water-supply/>
— the publisher's page for this methodology. Second links: <https://globalgoals.goldstandard.org/101-par-principles-requirements/>,
<https://globalgoals.goldstandard.org/201-ar-community-services-activity-requirements/>,
<https://globalgoals.goldstandard.org/501-pr-ghg-emissions-reductions-sequestration/>. None is a
mirror or a re-hosted copy.

**Applies to.** Every project claiming suppressed demand.

**External standard.** None; the tonnage lines are Gold Standard's.

**What a project owner would be asked to show.**

- A rough estimate of yearly emission reductions against the two scale lines.
- Whether the people counted boil today, or are counted under suppressed demand.

## Card M7 — Suppressed demand comes with basic service

**The rule in plain words.** A project counting suppressed demand has to give its users at least
"basic" drinking-water service on the WHO/UNICEF ladder: an improved source within a thirty-minute
round trip, queuing included. A household or institutional treatment project treating water from
an unimproved source qualifies if the water at the point of use reaches basic service; the bar is
about the service delivered, not the situation before. An institution already on a public network
that supplies safe water cannot count suppressed demand, unless that water is shown to fail the
safe-water definition.

**Can it be fixed? Yes.** Deliver basic service, which is a design choice; for an institution on a
network, test the network's water over six months or find literature no older than three years.
Route source: §3.2.4.3–3.2.4.4 and SDWS 12; planned carbon route R-4.

**Phase.** To start.

**Source.** GS4GG PAA M400-12 (Version 2.0, 9 July 2026), §3.2.4.3–3.2.4.4, pp. 10–11; Table 2,
"Minimum Service Quality Standard", p. 7; parameter SDWS 12, pp. 52–53.

**Canonical link.** <https://globalgoals.goldstandard.org/429-ee-sws-emission-reductions-from-safe-drinking-water-supply/>
— the publisher's page for this methodology. Not a mirror or a re-hosted copy.

**Applies to.** Every project claiming suppressed demand; the network rule for IWT and for
institutions served by a community system.

**External standard.** The WHO/UNICEF JMP service ladder: "basic" and "safely managed" as defined
in *Core questions on water, sanitation and hygiene for household surveys*, 2018 update, Table 1,
p. 7.

**What a project owner would be asked to show.**

- The service level the design delivers, in the ladder's words.
- For an institution on a network: the network water's tests or the literature.

## Card M8 — Collected water is within reach

**The rule in plain words.** Where people collect water rather than receive it piped, the source
has to be within one kilometre of their homes, or a thirty-minute round trip including queuing.
People beyond that line are not counted, unless the project shows, with evidence an auditor
accepts, that a wider reach fits national water policy for that kind of place, such as arid land or
a dispersed or nomadic population, and that those people really draw their drinking water there.

**Can it be fixed? Yes.** Count only the people within the line, or gather the policy evidence and
the surveys or GPS data for the wider reach. Route source: §3.2.5.1 b; planned carbon route R-5.

**Phase.** To start.

**Source.** GS4GG PAA M400-12 (Version 2.0, 9 July 2026), §3.2.5 "Accessibility", p. 11;
parameter SDWS 1, pp. 46–47.

**Canonical link.** <https://globalgoals.goldstandard.org/429-ee-sws-emission-reductions-from-safe-drinking-water-supply/>
— the publisher's page for this methodology. Not a mirror or a re-hosted copy.

**Applies to.** CWT, CWS, where water is collected.

**External standard.** National or regional water-supply policy, for the exception only.

**What a project owner would be asked to show.**

- The source's location and the homes it serves, with distances or round-trip times.
- For a wider reach: the policy it fits, and the surveys or GPS data.

## Card M9 — A restored system was really out of action

**The rule in plain words.** Restoring an existing community treatment or supply system counts
only if the system had been out of action for at least three months and nobody had a maintenance
or repair plan for it. The owner shows that with letters from local representatives or government,
the original installation date, and what was replaced, when, and by whom.

**Can it be fixed? Depends, on the facts of the outage.** The history cannot be changed; the
evidence for it can be gathered. A system that was working within the last three months, or that
had a repair plan, is not a restoration the method counts, and Blocked only when the visitor
confirms that. Route source: §3.2.6 and SDWS 2; planned carbon route R-6 for the plan going
forward.

**Phase.** To start.

**Source.** GS4GG PAA M400-12 (Version 2.0, 9 July 2026), §3.2.6 "Rehabilitation", p. 11;
Table 2, "Rehabilitation", p. 7; parameter SDWS 2, p. 47.

**Canonical link.** <https://globalgoals.goldstandard.org/429-ee-sws-emission-reductions-from-safe-drinking-water-supply/>
— the publisher's page for this methodology. Not a mirror or a re-hosted copy.

**Applies to.** CWT, CWS, where the system is restored rather than new.

**External standard.** None.

**What a project owner would be asked to show.**

- When the system stopped working, and letters saying so.
- That no repair plan existed; the original installation date; what was replaced.

## Card M10 — The technology's life is known, and replacement is planned

**The rule in plain words.** The technical life of the technology is stated from the maker's
specification, a third-party certification or the installer's guarantee, never from an expert's
opinion alone. Where that life is shorter than the crediting period, the project has a replacement
or retrofit plan with a performance guarantee; otherwise the claims stop when the technology's life
ends, and units past their life drop out of the count.

**Can it be fixed? Yes.** Get the specification or guarantee; write the replacement plan, or accept
claims that stop at the end of the life. Route source: §3.2.7 and SDWS 7; planned carbon route
R-7.

**Phase.** To start. Kept up by: the replacement or retrofit carried out when the technology's life ends.

**Source.** GS4GG PAA M400-12 (Version 2.0, 9 July 2026), §3.2.7 "Technical life and
replacement", p. 11; parameter SDWS 7, p. 50; Table 1, p. 5.

**Canonical link.** <https://globalgoals.goldstandard.org/429-ee-sws-emission-reductions-from-safe-drinking-water-supply/>
— the publisher's page for this methodology. Not a mirror or a re-hosted copy.

**Applies to.** All.

**External standard.** None here; the method also names an A6.4 tool for technical lifetime,
which this repository does not hold.

**What a project owner would be asked to show.**

- The technical life, with its source.
- The replacement or retrofit plan and its guarantee, where the life is shorter than the period.

## Card M11 — Nothing is counted twice

**The rule in plain words.** Every unit carries its own identifier in a project database, tracked
at least to the village or ward. No unit is already in another carbon scheme, and the project does
not displace another project's devices. Where a cookstove project serves the same homes, the
baseline assumes the efficient stove and the same fuel saving is never claimed twice. Where a
household project and an institutional project serve the same people, the water credited per
person stays under the age cap and the school's share is deducted. Where a jurisdictional forest
programme's reference level counts household fuelwood, the project shows its reductions are not
issued twice. Across Gold Standard, a project in another programme needs Gold Standard's leave.

**Can it be fixed? Yes.** Build the database, search the registries, survey the overlap, sign the
declaration; where a forest programme conflicts, get the accounting exclusion or a letter of no
objection. Route source: §3.2.8 and SDWS 18–19; planned carbon route R-8.

**Phase.** To start. Kept up by: the unit database kept current as units are added or replaced.

**Source.** GS4GG PAA M400-12 (Version 2.0, 9 July 2026), §3.2.8.1 a, b and e, pp. 11–12;
§3.2.8.2–3.2.8.4, pp. 12–13; parameters SDWS 18 and 19, pp. 55–56. Also *Principles &
Requirements* (v2.1, 31 January 2025), §3.1.1 c, p. 6.

**Canonical link.** <https://globalgoals.goldstandard.org/429-ee-sws-emission-reductions-from-safe-drinking-water-supply/>
— the publisher's page for this methodology. Second link:
<https://globalgoals.goldstandard.org/101-par-principles-requirements/>. Neither is a mirror or a
re-hosted copy.

**Applies to.** All.

**External standard.** Other carbon registries, for the search; the host country's forest
reference level, where one exists.

**What a project owner would be asked to show.**

- How units are distributed and identified.
- Registry searches; any overlap survey; the non-overlap declaration where a forest programme exists.

## Card M12 — No law already requires it, and the host country has not excluded it

**The rule in plain words.** The reductions must not already be required by an enforced law,
regulation or funded government scheme. Where a law exists but is not enforced, the project may
show that with evidence, except in a high-income country, where every law is treated as enforced.
And the host country must not have put this kind of activity on a list of activities excluded from
carbon markets. That is shown in one of three ways: citing the country's official list and finding
the activity not on it; a letter of authorisation or no objection from the country's authority; or,
where no list exists, a formal declaration that nothing has been issued excluding it. It is checked
when the project is first assessed, at a transition, and at each renewal.

**Can it be fixed? Depends, and one case ends Blocked.** An enforced law requiring the reductions:
Fixable only by evidence of non-enforcement, and not at all in a high-income country. No list and
no letter yet: Fixable, by the declaration. The country's list excludes this activity type:
Blocked, because only the country can change it and no cited route exists. Route source: §6.3.4
and §6.3.6; planned carbon route R-9.

**Phase.** To start. Kept up by: re-run at each renewal of the crediting period, and at once for a transitioning project.

**Source.** GS4GG PAA M400-12 (Version 2.0, 9 July 2026), §6.3.1–6.3.6 "Regulatory surplus" and
"Host country eligibility", pp. 19–21. Also *Requirements for Additionality Demonstration* (v1.0,
24 April 2025), §5.3.1 a, p. 7, and §6.1.1–6.1.4, p. 11; *PAA Validation and Verification
Requirements for Methodologies Update* (VVS100-01, v2.0, 10 July 2026), Annex 5, Table A5-1,
"Regulatory surplus", p. 45.

**Canonical link.** <https://globalgoals.goldstandard.org/429-ee-sws-emission-reductions-from-safe-drinking-water-supply/>
— the publisher's page for this methodology. Second links:
<https://globalgoals.goldstandard.org/447-requirements-for-additionality-demonstration/>,
<https://globalgoals.goldstandard.org/120_paa-vvs100-01-paa-validation-and-verification-requirements-for-methodologies-update/>.
None is a mirror or a re-hosted copy.

**Applies to.** All; a transitioning project re-runs it before any 2026 credit.

**External standard.** The host country's law, and its Article 6 framework, lists and authority.
Only a negative-list check is required by the held documents; whether any positive authorisation is
needed for a use of the credit is outside them, and the card says nothing beyond the check.

**What a project owner would be asked to show.**

- A summary of the laws and regulations that touch safe-water supply there.
- The list citation, the letter, or the declaration.

## Card M13 — Additional, by the positive list or by analysis

**The rule in plain words.** The project has to show it would not happen without carbon revenue,
one of two ways. Deemed additional, if all four positive-list conditions hold: a listed technology
(household or institutional filters, chlorination, UV, solar disinfection, or free community
dispensers); users who lack safely managed water, in a least-developed country or small island
state, or in another developing country where the users are below the poverty line and the
technology is below the common-practice line; no full cost recovery from users; and no law
requiring it. Otherwise an investment analysis or, for small projects, a barrier analysis. A
performance-based route is not allowed. The list is checked at the date of submission and reviewed
every three years, and a project that recovers its full cost or is viable without carbon finance
is off it.

**Can it be fixed? Depends, on the money.** Off the list for pricing: Fixable by keeping the
price below cost, or by the analysis. Off it for the country or the technology: the analysis.
Financially viable without carbon finance, and the visitor says the pricing will not change:
Blocked, because the standard's own definition of additional is not met. Route source: Annex 5
§A5.2 and §6.5–6.6; planned carbon routes R-10 and R-11.

**Phase.** To start. Kept up by: the ongoing-financial-need check at each renewal (§6.8, pp. 24–25).

**Source.** GS4GG PAA M400-12 (Version 2.0, 9 July 2026), §6.1.1–6.2.1, p. 19; §6.5–6.6,
pp. 22–23; Annex 5, §A5.2–A5.4, pp. 99–101. Also *Requirements for Additionality Demonstration*
(v1.0, 24 April 2025), §5.3.2–5.3.6, p. 8; *Principles & Requirements* (v2.1, 31 January 2025),
§4.1.46–4.1.47, p. 16; *Community Services Activity Requirements* (v1.2, October 2019), §4.1.9,
p. 6.

**Canonical link.** <https://globalgoals.goldstandard.org/429-ee-sws-emission-reductions-from-safe-drinking-water-supply/>
— the publisher's page for this methodology. Second links:
<https://globalgoals.goldstandard.org/447-requirements-for-additionality-demonstration/>,
<https://globalgoals.goldstandard.org/101-par-principles-requirements/>,
<https://globalgoals.goldstandard.org/201-ar-community-services-activity-requirements/>. None is
a mirror or a re-hosted copy.

**Applies to.** All.

**External standard.** The UN lists of least-developed countries and small island developing
states; the host country's national poverty line, or the World Bank's international line for its
income class; the World Bank income classification.

**What a project owner would be asked to show.**

- Which route: the four list conditions documented, or the analysis.
- Whether users pay, and whether what they pay covers the full cost.

## Card M14 — Not yet common practice

**The rule in plain words.** Every project, on the positive list or not, checks how common the
technology already is. The share of the target market, the people without verifiably safe water
in the host country or a justified smaller area, already using an equivalent technology has to be
below a quarter, leaving out units paid for by carbon or climate finance. The data can be no older
than five years. At or above a quarter, the technology is common practice and the project is not
additional. An older rule for community-services projects put the line at a fifth for safe-water
technologies that stop boiling.

**Can it be fixed? Depends, on the share and the area.** Above the line nationally but not in a
justified sub-national area, such as a rural district: Fixable, by the narrower area with its
justification. At or above the line with no such area: Blocked, because the standard says so and
no cited route changes the figure. Unknown, when nobody has the data: not a fail; the data is
what to find out. Route source: §6.7.2 c.

**Phase.** To start. Kept up by: re-run at each renewal of the crediting period.

**Source.** GS4GG PAA M400-12 (Version 2.0, 9 July 2026), §6.7.1–6.7.3 "Common practice
analysis", pp. 23–24. Also *Requirements for Additionality Demonstration* (v1.0, 24 April 2025),
§6.5, p. 16; *Community Services Activity Requirements* (v1.2, October 2019), Annex B §1.1.5 and
its footnote 16, pp. 16–17.

**Canonical link.** <https://globalgoals.goldstandard.org/429-ee-sws-emission-reductions-from-safe-drinking-water-supply/>
— the publisher's page for this methodology. Second links:
<https://globalgoals.goldstandard.org/447-requirements-for-additionality-demonstration/>,
<https://globalgoals.goldstandard.org/201-ar-community-services-activity-requirements/>. None is
a mirror or a re-hosted copy.

**Applies to.** All; a transitioning project re-runs it at the renewal of its crediting period.

**External standard.** None; the method names Gold Standard's own common-practice tool.

**What a project owner would be asked to show.**

- The share of the target market already using an equivalent technology, and the data's date.
- Where a smaller area is used, why it differs from the country.

## Card M15 — No lock-in

**The rule in plain words.** The project must not lock in emissions or technology at odds with the
host country's climate strategy. A zero-emission technology, such as a gravity filter, a handpump or
a solar pump, passes outright, whatever its life. A technology with a life under ten years, such as
a ceramic filter, a membrane or UV unit or a chlorine dispenser, passes if it fits the country's
sustainable-energy strategy, for projects submitted by the end of 2030. A grid-electric system
passes only with the maker's evidence of a life of ten years or less. Anything else goes through
the full lock-in assessment; a fossil back-up within its tenth does not trigger it.

**Can it be fixed? Yes.** Show the technology's life with the maker's evidence, or state how it
fits the country's strategy, or run the full assessment. Route source: §6.4.2–6.4.3; planned
carbon route R-12.

**Phase.** To start.

**Source.** GS4GG PAA M400-12 (Version 2.0, 9 July 2026), §6.4.1–6.4.4 "Lock-in risk analysis",
pp. 21–22. Also *Requirements for Additionality Demonstration* (v1.0, 24 April 2025),
§6.2.1–6.2.5, pp. 11–12.

**Canonical link.** <https://globalgoals.goldstandard.org/429-ee-sws-emission-reductions-from-safe-drinking-water-supply/>
— the publisher's page for this methodology. Second link:
<https://globalgoals.goldstandard.org/447-requirements-for-additionality-demonstration/>. Neither
is a mirror or a re-hosted copy.

**Applies to.** All; a transitioning project re-runs it before any 2026 credit.

**External standard.** The host country's long-term low-emission strategy, where it has one.

**What a project owner would be asked to show.**

- What the technology is and what powers it; its life, with the maker's evidence.
- Where it is not zero-emission and not short-lived, the assessment.

## Card M16 — Groundwater is drawn within what the aquifer can give

**The rule in plain words.** A borehole or other groundwater point that draws more than five cubic
metres a day, averaged over the year, needs a hydrogeological assessment, or a permit from the
national water authority, confirming the draw is within the aquifer's sustainable yield. At or
below five a day is exempt. The safeguarding principles say the same in general terms: groundwater
abstraction not above recharge.

**Can it be fixed? Yes.** Get the assessment or the permit, or design the draw at or below the
line. Route source: §3.4.7; planned carbon route R-13.

**Phase.** To start.

**Source.** GS4GG PAA M400-12 (Version 2.0, 9 July 2026), §3.4.7 "Water resource competition",
p. 14. Also *Safeguarding Principles & Requirements* (v2.1, 29 June 2023), Principle 8.2,
pp. 27–28.

**Canonical link.** <https://globalgoals.goldstandard.org/429-ee-sws-emission-reductions-from-safe-drinking-water-supply/>
— the publisher's page for this methodology. Second link:
<https://globalgoals.goldstandard.org/103-par-safeguarding-principles-requirements/>. Neither is
a mirror or a re-hosted copy.

**Applies to.** CWS, where the source is groundwater.

**External standard.** The national water authority, for the permit. The methodology's supplement
words the line as "at or above" and says grouped handpumps count together; the methodology
governs, so exactly five a day is exempt (Summary, p. 1).

**What a project owner would be asked to show.**

- The expected draw per point, averaged over the year.
- The assessment or the permit, where the draw is above the line.

## Card M17 — The running rules are in the design

**The rule in plain words.** The project documents the regulations that touch safe-water supply,
operation, maintenance and tariffs there, and does not undermine them. A community treatment or
supply system has an ongoing maintenance and repair plan, written into the design, with a log of
operation, maintenance and downtime kept for monitoring. And the project runs a water-hygiene
education campaign every year. The method also asks for the WHO water-safety-plan cycle, safe
handling of chemicals, and sound disposal of used filters and containers.

**Can it be fixed? Yes.** Write the regulatory summary, the maintenance plan and the campaign into
the design. Route source: §3.4.2–3.4.3 and §3.2.3.3; planned carbon route R-6.

**Phase.** To remain eligible. For a planned project this row shows in the preview block, never as a mark against it; the plan and the campaign are commitments a planned project makes, and the water pack's consideration B-2, the implementer is ready and able, is the one question that settles it.

**Source.** GS4GG PAA M400-12 (Version 2.0, 9 July 2026), §3.4.2–3.4.3, p. 13; §3.4.4–3.4.6,
pp. 13–14; §3.2.3.3, p. 10; parameters SDWS 4, pp. 48–49, and SDWS 24, pp. 59–60.

**Canonical link.** <https://globalgoals.goldstandard.org/429-ee-sws-emission-reductions-from-safe-drinking-water-supply/>
— the publisher's page for this methodology. Not a mirror or a re-hosted copy.

**Applies to.** All; the maintenance plan and log for CWT and CWS.

**External standard.** The national regulations on water supply and tariffs; the WHO water safety
plan approach, which the method names without a citation.

**What a project owner would be asked to show.**

- The regulatory summary.
- The maintenance and repair plan; the campaign plan.
