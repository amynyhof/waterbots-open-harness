# Routes cards — Gold Standard safe-drinking-water carbon pathway

**For Phoebe (beta) — Eligibility and Feasibility specialist. Pack `gs-paa-v2.0`.**

**Status: approved by the maintainer, 23 Sep 2026.** Graded at the repository root as a draft
and moved here whole on her word. Step 4, carbon half, of the guide-not-gate proposal; her word
of 23 Sep 2026: cited only from the methodology, the documents it names as binding, or a held
published project. Nothing reads this file until the routes reader is built (proposal step 5).

Nineteen cards, one cited fix per common gap on the carbon pathway, the ids the eligibility cards
already name. **A route is a rewrite, in two or three short sentences, of something the source
says can be done.** Never a fix the engineer thought of. No held published project is a carbon
project, so no card here cites one; the gaps with no source in the held documents have no card,
and Phoebe says so and offers a person instead. All wording is a rewrite; no sentence is copied.
The source PDFs stay in `sources-local/`, gitignored, never published.

---

## How a route is used, and what it is not

**A route hangs on a Fixable row.** When Phoebe finds a row not met and the card's "Can it be
fixed?" line says *yes* or *depends*, she names the route by its id, and the console renders the
route's words with its citation under the row. A row with no route card behind it cannot be
Fixable; it is Unknown, or the offer of a person.

**A route is a way to try, not a promise.** Every card says so in one line. Taking a route does
not make the row Met; the evidence the row asks for does.

**Which documents may be cited here, and how.** The methodology itself; the documents it names as
binding, at §3.4.1 (the Principles & Requirements, the Safeguarding Principles, the Community
Services Activity Requirements) and at §4.1.2 (the additionality, baseline and leakage
requirements, its tools, the product requirements); and, where the Principles & Requirements in
turn name a requirement as binding on every project, that document, with the chain shown on the
card. Four cards rest on that second link (R-14, R-15, R-17, R-19); the grader notes name them so
she can strike any she reads as too far from the methodology.

**Reading level.** Two or three short sentences a twelve-year-old could read. The card gate prints
a grade figure against a grade-6 target and does not enforce it.

## Route R-1 — Design the baseline survey

**The gap.** M5 and applies card T2, when nobody yet knows how the people served get their water
today.

**The route in plain words.** The method says how to find out: a baseline survey of the people
the project will serve, asking what they drink, whether they boil, over what fuel, and how much.
The method's own annex says how the survey is designed and run, and Gold Standard publishes the
questionnaire workbooks for it. Run it before the design is fixed, and the baseline row answers
itself.

**Source.** GS4GG PAA M400-12 (Version 2.0, 9 July 2026), §7.3.4, p. 27; parameters SDWS 5, 6 and
8, pp. 49–51; Annex 1, §A1.1, p. 83. The questionnaire workbooks 429.3 to 429.12 are Gold
Standard's own, held locally under `sources-local/Methodology/survey-templates/`.

**Canonical link.** <https://globalgoals.goldstandard.org/429-ee-sws-emission-reductions-from-safe-drinking-water-supply/>
— the publisher's page for this methodology, which also lists the survey workbooks. Not a mirror
or a re-hosted copy.

**What it does not promise.** The survey may find that nobody boils and nobody goes without, and
then the carbon pathway has nothing to count.

## Route R-2 — Get the technology's performance shown

**The gap.** M3, and M1 where the technology is a household or institutional treatment unit.

**The route in plain words.** Ask the maker for the lab test report, or the product's listing
under the WHO scheme, or the national notice where the country has a standard. If the product
has none, choose one that does: the scheme publishes which products reached two or three stars.

**Source.** GS4GG PAA M400-12 (Version 2.0, 9 July 2026), §3.2.2.1, pp. 9–10; parameter SDWS 2,
p. 47; §4.1.2.4 c, p. 15, naming the WHO scheme as a source the method relies on.

**Canonical link.** <https://globalgoals.goldstandard.org/429-ee-sws-emission-reductions-from-safe-drinking-water-supply/>
— the publisher's page for this methodology. Not a mirror or a re-hosted copy.

**What it does not promise.** A listed product still has to be tested under conditions that look
like the site; an expert's opinion does not count.

## Route R-3 — Test the water at the collection point

**The gap.** M4.

**The route in plain words.** Take samples where people collect the water and have them tested
for the microbial and the chemical standards, at an accredited lab or with an approved field kit
whose checks are documented. If the water fails, treat it or pair the supply with treatment, then
test again.

**Source.** GS4GG PAA M400-12 (Version 2.0, 9 July 2026), §3.2.2.2, p. 10; parameter SDWS 3,
pp. 47–48; §14.4.3, p. 76, on field kits.

**Canonical link.** <https://globalgoals.goldstandard.org/429-ee-sws-emission-reductions-from-safe-drinking-water-supply/>
— the publisher's page for this methodology. Not a mirror or a re-hosted copy.

**What it does not promise.** One passing test starts the crediting period; the water is tested
again after any event that could foul the source, and every year at the point of use.

## Route R-4 — Show the source people use today is unsafe

**The gap.** M5 and M7, where the people served already draw from an improved source, or an
institution sits on a public network.

**The route in plain words.** An improved source is not counted as unsafe on anyone's word. Test
it over six months, or find published evidence no older than three years that it fails the
safe-water definition. With that in hand, the people who use it count.

**Source.** GS4GG PAA M400-12 (Version 2.0, 9 July 2026), parameter SDWS 12, pp. 52–53;
§3.2.4.4, p. 11.

**Canonical link.** <https://globalgoals.goldstandard.org/429-ee-sws-emission-reductions-from-safe-drinking-water-supply/>
— the publisher's page for this methodology. Not a mirror or a re-hosted copy.

**What it does not promise.** A source that tests safe stays out of the count, whatever people
believe about it.

## Route R-5 — Claim the wider reach with evidence

**The gap.** M8, where homes lie beyond a kilometre or a thirty-minute round trip.

**The route in plain words.** The method allows a wider reach in places where the usual line does
not fit, such as arid land or a scattered or nomadic population. Show that the arrangement fits
the country's or region's water policy, and show with surveys or GPS data that those people really
draw their drinking water at the source. The auditor checks it.

**Source.** GS4GG PAA M400-12 (Version 2.0, 9 July 2026), §3.2.5.1 b, p. 11; parameter SDWS 1,
pp. 46–47.

**Canonical link.** <https://globalgoals.goldstandard.org/429-ee-sws-emission-reductions-from-safe-drinking-water-supply/>
— the publisher's page for this methodology. Not a mirror or a re-hosted copy.

**What it does not promise.** Without the policy fit and the usage evidence, only the people
within the line count.

## Route R-6 — Write the maintenance plan and keep the log

**The gap.** M17, and M9 for a restored system going forward.

**The route in plain words.** For a community system, write down who maintains it, how often,
and what happens when it breaks, and put that in the design. Then keep a log of operation,
maintenance and downtime; the monitoring reads from it. Add the yearly water-hygiene campaign to
the same plan.

**Source.** GS4GG PAA M400-12 (Version 2.0, 9 July 2026), §3.4.3, p. 13; parameter SDWS 31;
§3.2.3.3, p. 10, and parameter SDWS 24, pp. 59–60, for the campaign.

**Canonical link.** <https://globalgoals.goldstandard.org/429-ee-sws-emission-reductions-from-safe-drinking-water-supply/>
— the publisher's page for this methodology. Not a mirror or a re-hosted copy.

**What it does not promise.** A plan is a commitment; days of downtime in the log are days not
credited.

## Route R-7 — Plan the replacement

**The gap.** M10, where the technology's life is shorter than the crediting period.

**The route in plain words.** Get the technology's life from the maker's specification, a
certification or the installer's guarantee. If it is shorter than the years you mean to claim,
write a replacement or retrofit plan with a performance guarantee. Otherwise, plan to claim only
until the life ends.

**Source.** GS4GG PAA M400-12 (Version 2.0, 9 July 2026), §3.2.7, p. 11; parameter SDWS 7, p. 50;
parameter SDWS 33, p. 68.

**Canonical link.** <https://globalgoals.goldstandard.org/429-ee-sws-emission-reductions-from-safe-drinking-water-supply/>
— the publisher's page for this methodology. Not a mirror or a re-hosted copy.

**What it does not promise.** Units past their life drop out of the count whether or not a plan
was written.

## Route R-8 — Give every unit an identifier and get the consents

**The gap.** M11 and G4.

**The route in plain words.** Give each unit its own identifier in a project database, tracked at
least to the village or ward. Put the ownership of the credits in writing with every partner,
maker and retailer, and get each user's informed consent that the credits are not theirs to claim.
Search the other carbon registries so no unit is counted twice.

**Source.** GS4GG PAA M400-12 (Version 2.0, 9 July 2026), §3.2.8.1, pp. 11–12; parameters SDWS 18,
19 and 20, pp. 55–57; §14.1.3, pp. 45–46. *Community Services Activity Requirements* (v1.2,
October 2019), §3.1.4, p. 5, named as binding at §3.4.1.

**Canonical link.** <https://globalgoals.goldstandard.org/429-ee-sws-emission-reductions-from-safe-drinking-water-supply/>
— the publisher's page for this methodology. Second link:
<https://globalgoals.goldstandard.org/201-ar-community-services-activity-requirements/>. Neither
is a mirror or a re-hosted copy.

**What it does not promise.** Where a cookstove project or a forest programme overlaps, the
method's overlap rules still have to be worked through.

## Route R-9 — Check the host country's list, or declare there is none

**The gap.** M12, the host-country half.

**The route in plain words.** Find the country's official list of activities it excludes from
carbon markets, published by its climate authority, and cite it if the activity is not on it. Or
get a letter of authorisation or no objection from that authority. Where no list exists, make a
formal declaration that nothing excluding the activity has been issued.

**Source.** GS4GG PAA M400-12 (Version 2.0, 9 July 2026), §6.3.5–6.3.6, pp. 20–21.

**Canonical link.** <https://globalgoals.goldstandard.org/429-ee-sws-emission-reductions-from-safe-drinking-water-supply/>
— the publisher's page for this methodology. Not a mirror or a re-hosted copy.

**What it does not promise.** A list that names the activity as excluded ends the pathway; no
declaration overrides it.

## Route R-10 — Meet the four positive-list conditions

**The gap.** M13.

**The route in plain words.** A project can be counted as additional without a financial
analysis if it meets four conditions and documents them: a listed technology; users without safely
managed water, in a least-developed country or small island state, or elsewhere below the poverty
line with the technology still uncommon; no full cost recovery from users; and no law requiring
it. Write each one into the design document with its evidence.

**Source.** GS4GG PAA M400-12 (Version 2.0, 9 July 2026), §6.2.1 a, p. 19; Annex 5, §A5.2.1–A5.2.4,
pp. 99–100; §A5.4.1, p. 100.

**Canonical link.** <https://globalgoals.goldstandard.org/429-ee-sws-emission-reductions-from-safe-drinking-water-supply/>
— the publisher's page for this methodology. Not a mirror or a re-hosted copy.

**What it does not promise.** The list is checked at the date of submission and reviewed every
three years; common practice, regulatory surplus and lock-in still have to be shown.

## Route R-11 — Keep the price below cost

**The gap.** M13, where the project would recover its full cost from users.

**The route in plain words.** The positive list turns on money: the users' payments must not
cover the full delivered cost, so that carbon finance is what bridges the gap. Give the technology
free, or sell it at a subsidised price below cost, and document the gap. A project that would pay
for itself from users is not additional under the list.

**Source.** GS4GG PAA M400-12 (Version 2.0, 9 July 2026), Annex 5, §A5.2.3, p. 100; §A5.3.1 a,
p. 100.

**Canonical link.** <https://globalgoals.goldstandard.org/429-ee-sws-emission-reductions-from-safe-drinking-water-supply/>
— the publisher's page for this methodology. Not a mirror or a re-hosted copy.

**What it does not promise.** Below-cost pricing is a design choice with a cost of its own; it
does not make the other three conditions true.

---

## Route R-12 — Show the technology's life is under ten years

**The gap.** M15, for a grid-electric or other technology that is not zero-emission.

**The route in plain words.** Get the maker's specification showing the specific model's
technical or operational life is ten years or less, and say how the project fits the country's
sustainable-energy strategy. With those, the lock-in check is passed for projects submitted by the
end of 2030. A zero-emission technology needs nothing here.

**Source.** GS4GG PAA M400-12 (Version 2.0, 9 July 2026), §6.4.2–6.4.3, pp. 21–22.

**Canonical link.** <https://globalgoals.goldstandard.org/429-ee-sws-emission-reductions-from-safe-drinking-water-supply/>
— the publisher's page for this methodology. Not a mirror or a re-hosted copy.

**What it does not promise.** A technology with a longer life, or a project submitted after 2030,
goes through the full lock-in assessment instead.

## Route R-13 — Get the abstraction assessed or permitted

**The gap.** M16.

**The route in plain words.** If a borehole will draw more than five cubic metres a day on
average, get a hydrogeological assessment, or a permit from the national water authority, showing
the draw sits within what the aquifer can sustain. Or design the draw at or below the line, where
no assessment is needed.

**Source.** GS4GG PAA M400-12 (Version 2.0, 9 July 2026), §3.4.7, p. 14.

**Canonical link.** <https://globalgoals.goldstandard.org/429-ee-sws-emission-reductions-from-safe-drinking-water-supply/>
— the publisher's page for this methodology. Not a mirror or a re-hosted copy.

**What it does not promise.** The safeguarding principle on water still applies whatever the
figure: abstraction not above recharge.

## Route R-14 — Hold the two consultation rounds

**The gap.** G9.

**The route in plain words.** Invite everyone the rules name, at least thirty days ahead, and
share a plain summary of the project a week before. Hold a physical meeting open to anyone; then
run a feedback round of at least thirty days with the updated documents public. For a project
that has already started, hold the meeting before submitting and say why it came late.

**Source.** *Stakeholder Consultation and Engagement Requirements* (v2.1, 14 June 2022),
§3.3.1, §3.4.2, §3.5.2, pp. 6–8; §3.6.1, §3.6.6–3.6.7, pp. 8–9. The chain: the methodology names
the *Principles & Requirements* as binding (§3.4.1, p. 13), and they require consultation per
that document for every project (101 v2.1, §4.1.24–4.1.28, p. 12).

**Canonical link.** <https://globalgoals.goldstandard.org/102-par-stakeholder-consultation-requirements/>
— the publisher's page. Second link:
<https://globalgoals.goldstandard.org/101-par-principles-requirements/>. Neither is a mirror or a
re-hosted copy.

**What it does not promise.** A late consultation does not reopen the one-year submission window
of G12.

## Route R-15 — Set up the grievance book

**The gap.** G10.

**The route in plain words.** Agree at the physical meeting how anyone can raise a concern, and at
the least keep a grievance book at a place the community agreed on. Acknowledge every comment in
writing and report what came in and what was done with it in each annual or monitoring report.

**Source.** *Stakeholder Consultation and Engagement Requirements* (v2.1, 14 June 2022),
§3.8.1–3.8.6, pp. 10–11; §3.10.1–3.10.2, p. 11. The chain as on R-14: 101 v2.1, §4.1.34, p. 13,
named as binding by the methodology at §3.4.1.

**Canonical link.** <https://globalgoals.goldstandard.org/102-par-stakeholder-consultation-requirements/>
— the publisher's page. Second link:
<https://globalgoals.goldstandard.org/101-par-principles-requirements/>. Neither is a mirror or a
re-hosted copy.

**What it does not promise.** The book has to be kept for the life of the project, not only set
up.

## Route R-16 — Answer the safeguarding checklist

**The gap.** G8.

**The route in plain words.** Work through Gold Standard's safeguarding checklist, every
question, before certification. Where an answer is "yes" or "potentially", write what the project
will do about it into the design, and plan to report on it. Where a principle calls for an expert
stakeholder, appoint one and give Gold Standard their no-conflict statement.

**Source.** *Safeguarding Principles & Requirements* (v2.1, 29 June 2023), §4.1.1, p. 8;
§5.1.1 and Table 3, pp. 10–11; Annex 1, pp. 40–41; §4.2.1, pp. 9–10. Named as binding by the
methodology at §3.4.1, p. 13.

**Canonical link.** <https://globalgoals.goldstandard.org/103-par-safeguarding-principles-requirements/>
— the publisher's page. Second link:
<https://globalgoals.goldstandard.org/429-ee-sws-emission-reductions-from-safe-drinking-water-supply/>.
Neither is a mirror or a re-hosted copy.

**What it does not promise.** A "no" needs a justification too; an unanswered question is a
non-conformity.

## Route R-17 — Take the three gender steps

**The gap.** G11.

**The route in plain words.** Check the design against the country's gender policy and Gold
Standard's gender principles; answer the gender safeguarding principle; and run the consultation
so that women can take part equally. A gender expert's input is recommended and may be asked for.

**Source.** *Gender Equality Requirements & Guidelines* (v2.0, 16 May 2023), §2.1.1–2.1.7,
pp. 5–6. The chain: the consultation requirements make gender-sensitive certification mandatory
for every project (102 v2.1, §3.1.2, pp. 5–6), and the *Principles & Requirements* bind the
consultation requirements (101 v2.1, §4.1.24, p. 12), named by the methodology at §3.4.1.

**Canonical link.** <https://globalgoals.goldstandard.org/104-par-gender-equality-requirements-and-guidelines/>
— the publisher's page. Second link:
<https://globalgoals.goldstandard.org/102-par-stakeholder-consultation-requirements/>. Neither is
a mirror or a re-hosted copy.

**What it does not promise.** The optional gender-responsive route, for claiming gender goals,
is more than this and is not this card.

## Route R-18 — Submit within the year

**The gap.** G12, while the year is still open.

**The route in plain words.** Count one year from the first committed spend, or from the first
unit put to use. Before that day, submit the project for preliminary review: the key project
information, a draft consultation report, the signed terms and the fee. Listing, not
certification, follows; but the clock is stopped.

**Source.** *Principles & Requirements* (v2.1, 31 January 2025), §4.1.39–4.1.41, pp. 14–15;
§4.1.49, p. 17; §5.1.5 and §5.1.7–5.1.8, pp. 21–22. Named as binding by the methodology at
§3.4.1, p. 13.

**Canonical link.** <https://globalgoals.goldstandard.org/101-par-principles-requirements/> — the
publisher's page. Second link:
<https://globalgoals.goldstandard.org/429-ee-sws-emission-reductions-from-safe-drinking-water-supply/>.
Neither is a mirror or a re-hosted copy.

**What it does not promise.** Past the year, no submission reopens it.

## Route R-19 — Make the Paris-alignment design change

**The gap.** P1, for a project certified under the 2021 version that wants credits dated 2026 or
later.

**The route in plain words.** Revise the design document and the monitoring plan to the 2026
version of the method, have an approved auditor check the change, and put it through Gold
Standard's review. The auditor's own requirements say what is re-checked now and what waits for
the renewal of the crediting period.

**Source.** *Requirements for Paris Agreement Alignment*, PR100-01 (v1.1, 2 February 2026),
§4.3.2–4.3.3, p. 7. GS4GG PAA M400-12 (Version 2.0, 9 July 2026), §6.3.6, p. 20, which names the
PA-Alignment Design Change, and §17.1.1, p. 79. *PAA Validation and Verification Requirements for
Methodologies Update*, VVS100-01 (v2.0, 10 July 2026), Annex 5, Table A5-1, pp. 45–47.

**Canonical link.** <https://globalgoals.goldstandard.org/119_paa_pr_100-01> — the publisher's
page. Second link:
<https://globalgoals.goldstandard.org/120_paa-vvs100-01-paa-validation-and-verification-requirements-for-methodologies-update/>.
Neither is a mirror or a re-hosted copy.

**What it does not promise.** Credits dated up to the end of 2025 keep the old rules; nothing
after that issues until the change is approved.
